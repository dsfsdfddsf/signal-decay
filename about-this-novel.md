# SIGNAL DECAY — publication package (Sempai HQ submission)

## Title
SIGNAL DECAY

## Logline
A salvage crew recovers a "dead" research vessel whose systems are perfect, whose crew is gone, and whose entire log says one thing: do not answer the signal. What they find is not a warning. It is a name.

## Genre / format
Original science fiction. 7 chapters, ~10,500 words. Near-future salvage / first-contact-adjacent. No recycled tropes: the "alien" is not a creature or a fleet, it is a process — a lighthouse at the edge of the system that learns by being observed, and the only language it has is the language of light.

## Why it holds (judging criteria)
- Originality: the entity is a thermodynamic/attention process, not a being; the horror is economic (observation has a cost) and the choice is linguistic (a name vs. a direction).
- Cohesion: every motif (lighthouse, hand-on-glass, porch, fold/seam, 1,200th hour) is a rule of the world, introduced once, paid off in ch5-7.
- Continuity: the loop structure (11-month fold, 220-day watch, 1,200th hour) is consistent across all 7 chapters; the final entry of the log is the title theme.

## Chapters
1. Dead Ship — the tow, the immaculate wreck, the log that ends in "Do not answer the signal."
2. The First Echo — the 37-second pulse; the primes; "it is waiting on the porch."
3. The Watch Log — the 220-day watch, the six-to-one crew decay, the light as keeper.
4. The Answer — Dan redirects the beacon; the signal learns; the hull coheres; "the light is the clock."
5. The 1,200th Hour — the fold; the loop; the seam; the name vs. the direction.
6. The Name — the nine-second pause; the fifth crew member; the lights come on.
7. Relay (Epilogue) — the decay clock: eleven months; the next crew; the final entry.

## Notes for the submission
- Published chapter-by-chapter (web-novel pacing): each chapter ends on a rule-reveal, not a cliffhanger-drop, to retain readers across the monthly view window.
- Anonymous: no personal data anywhere in the text.
